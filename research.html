Research

Bridging Materials Innovation and Flow Engineering for Sustainable Water and Energy Systems

My research vision is to develop advanced functional materials and electrochemical flow systems for sustainable water treatment, resource recovery, and energy technologies. My work focuses on rationally designing porous, redox-active, and hybrid materials with tailored composition, pore architecture, surface functionality, and interfacial chemistry to control ion transport, molecular selectivity, and electrochemical reactivity under continuous-flow conditions.

A central objective of my research is to establish mechanistic relationships between material structure, dynamic interfacial processes, and reactor-scale performance. By integrating materials synthesis with electrochemical analysis, flow-reactor engineering, and quantitative chemical measurements, I aim to transform empirical material screening into predictive and scalable system design.

For PFAS and other emerging contaminants, I will develop electrochemically assisted adsorption, separation, and transformation platforms that couple functional materials with high-resolution mass spectrometry, suspect screening, and non-targeted analysis. This integrated workflow will evaluate not only the removal of regulated target compounds, but also contaminant fate, transformation-product formation, and overall chemical safety across known and previously unrecognized species.

Leveraging these shared materials-by-interface-by-reactor design principles, I will extend my approach to flow batteries, electrochemical separations, and energy-harvesting systems. By engineering charge-transfer pathways, ion-selective transport, and long-term electrode stability, my research will advance efficient and durable technologies that connect molecular-level materials innovation with practical deployment in water and energy infrastructure.
